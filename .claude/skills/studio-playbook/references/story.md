# Story and visual storytelling

## Before cutting: the thesis

Write one sentence: **"This edit must make [viewer] [believe / feel / do] [X]."** Every beat either serves it or goes.
Then split the material into hook · setup · proof · payoff · CTA, and pick one signature visual move for the video.

## Hook archetypes (first 3 s)

| Hook | Line shape | On screen at frame 0 |
|---|---|---|
| Contrarian | "X is dead." / "Stop doing X." | the claim word giant (behind the subject if cut out), strike-through on the old belief |
| Number | "This took my score from 650 to 750." | the number, counting up or slammed in |
| Result first | "Here's the edit Claude made." | the after state, then rewind to the before |
| Question | "Does paying early hurt your score?" | the question as a card; answer withheld until the payoff |
| List promise | "3 settings that..." | a numbered stack with the first item lit |
| POV / mistake | "You're doing X wrong." | the wrong version labelled, a red mark |
| In medias res | a story mid-action | the most visual moment, then context |

## Structures

- **Claim → proof → payoff → CTA** (house B): each claim acted out literally, proof as real sources.
- **Before → after** (house A): split cards, slider or wipe; the after gets the accent colour.
- **Problem → agitate → solve:** the problem shown big, the cost made concrete with a number, then the fix.
- **List:** every item gets the same treatment (same position, entrance and sound) so the count is the rhythm.
- **Tutorial** (house C): one step per overlay, copyable cards held 3–5 s.
- **Demo montage** (house D): UI doing things, cut to the beat, keyword CTA end card.

## Visual storytelling

- **Picture says what the voice says, on the same word.** Numbers, names, lists and literal verbs get visuals;
  abstractions stay plain talking.
- **One focal point per frame.** The eye goes to faces, motion, contrast and text, in that order: design for one at a time.
- **Progressive disclosure:** reveal list items and chart parts as they are spoken, never all at once.
- **Callbacks:** a motif from the hook returns at the payoff (colour, word, shape). It makes the ending feel planned.
- **Concrete over abstract:** a $ figure, a date, a screenshot beats an icon of "money" or "time".
- **Emotional arc by craft:** tighter cuts and brighter grade for energy; held shots, space and quieter sound for weight.

## Proof

Real screenshots (captured at phone width, highlighted with the browser's own highlight), source cards with the
publisher and date, real charts from real numbers, the user's own results. Label a rebuilt UI as illustrative.
Never invent a metric, follower count, testimonial or comment.

## CTA

Specific and earned: "Comment 'EDIT' and I'll send the walkthrough" beats "like and subscribe". One CTA, at most 2 s,
on screen and spoken. A loopable last line ("...and that's why cold calling isn't dead") helps replays.

## On-screen copy

At most 6 words per line and 2 lines per card; digits for numbers ($10,000, 3x, 90%); sentence case, tracked caps only
for small labels; one accent word per card. Correct names from memory before captions (ASR mishears names).
