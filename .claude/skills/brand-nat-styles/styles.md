# Brand Nat Style Reference

Every style has a code. Mix codes freely, for example: `H10 C9 L4 X1 M1 S2`.
A visual version with a sketch of every style is in `guide/index.html`.

## H · Hook

The first 1 to 3 seconds. Fully on screen from frame 1.

- **H1 Chunky title behind head**: Small white italic line plus a big yellow title with espresso outline, sitting behind you (you're cut out in front).
- **H2 Sticker hook with logos**: Stacked white sticker pills, thick black border, brand-coloured names and each brand's logo. Best for comparisons.
- **H3 Warm typewriter caps**: Stacked cream caps with an espresso outline, a caramel subtitle line and emoji stickers. Warm and friendly.
- **H4 Scrapbook stack**: Thin italic serif line over a bold rounded word. Soft, editorial, lowercase.
- **H5 Title box, 3 seconds**: One flat coloured box with the hook, gone by 3s. Then the frame is clean.
- **H6 Hook filmed in the result**: Say the hook from inside the outcome (the seat, the lounge, the event). The place proves the claim before you explain it.
- **H7 Logo-pop question**: Your spoken question on screen from frame 1, just below the top. Brand logos pop in together on the first brand word. Text leaves on the first scene change.
- **H8 Handwritten label + bold lowercase**: Tiny handwritten caps line ("the trick to") over a bold lowercase title ("editing with claude.") on a flat yellow canvas, your clip as a card under it with a real comment bubble.
- **H9 Crossed-out logo vs logo**: Two app icons side by side up top, the old tool's logo struck through with a red X, the new one clean. Says "this replaces that" with no words.
- **H10 Two-weight title**: Heavy bold caps line over a thin condensed serif caps line ("A PRODUCTIVE / DAY WITH AI"), centred up top from frame 1, lifts away after about 3 seconds.

## C · Captions

How your words show up. Never over your face.

- **C1 Phrases + yellow keyword**: Hand-written phrases in sentence case, words reveal as you speak, one glowing yellow keyword per phrase.
- **C2 One bold word**: Big white caps, one word at a time, soft shadow. Punchy and fast.
- **C3 No captions**: Clean frame, only titles and graphics. Good for calm or premium pieces.
- **C4 Small + BIG keyword**: Small lowercase words with one big bold keyword in the accent colour. Most lines stay small.
- **C5 Phrases + white pill**: Short phrases, words pop as spoken, one keyword in a white pill. Wraps to two lines inside the TikTok safe zone and moves off products, logos and screens.
- **C6 White line + yellow punch line**: Small lowercase white line, then the punch words blur in underneath, bigger and in yellow. Whole caption blurs out as it leaves.
- **C7 Tracked caps + big serif**: Tiny wide-spaced caps line, then the punch word in a big serif (italic on the last word), letters rising from a mask. Editorial, expensive.
- **C8 Small caps under the chin**: One short all-caps line, small, sitting just under your chin. Barely there, keeps the UI above you readable.
- **C9 Staircase captions**: Words land one by one and step diagonally down the open space like stairs, 3 to 5 lines, then clear for the next phrase. Tight white bold sans for the small words, a big ice-tinted serif italic for the keyword with an underline that draws in. Placed wherever the frame is empty, never on your face.

## L · Layout

How you and the graphics share the frame.

- **L1 Depth cutout**: You're cut out and stand in front of floating cards, logos and titles. The room behind blurs.
- **L2 Split screen**: Proof or b-roll on top, you on the bottom half, hard cuts between.
- **L3 Scrapbook canvas**: Soft blue canvas, you cut out, tilted white-border video cards and stickers.
- **L4 Full frame + floating**: Your normal shot, graphics float in the clear space above you.
- **L5 Top-band story**: You in the bottom two thirds, a clean band on top for quotes, archive photos and proof. Face always visible.
- **L6 Headroom whiteboard**: Frame low, all text lives in the empty wall above your head. Nothing on your body.
- **L7 Three-layout rotation**: Rotate split with proof on top, face with one big word, and full-screen proof. Switch on every new idea.
- **L8 Polaroid narrator**: The screen (Claude window, before clip) is the main card and you talk from a small tilted polaroid in the corner. Great for showing a process.
- **L9 Before / After side by side**: Small labelled Before card on the left, big After card on the right, both playing, with an "Edited by" header on a soft patterned canvas.

## P · Proof

Shows the claim is real. Real sources, real screens.

- **P1 Article card + highlighter**: Real article screenshot zooms to the exact quote, yellow highlighter sweeps it, source and date pill.
- **P2 Official demo footage**: 2 to 5 second clips from the company's own launch video, labelled.
- **P3 Giant count-up number**: A big number counts up behind your head on the stat.
- **P4 Social post card**: The actual post (X, Threads, LinkedIn) floating as a card.
- **P6 Personal archive**: Your real old videos and photos on the matching line (first videos, your team, events). The strongest human signal you have.
- **P7 Cursor proof**: The real tool or table, screen recorded, with a cursor pointing at the number. Small source label.
- **P8 Finger on the screen**: Phone-filmed laptop or monitor with your finger pointing at your real balance or result.

## V · Visual beats

Visuals that explain or prove a line. Not one on every line.

- **V1 Logo tiles with roles**: Official logos pop in on the word with a one-word role (reasoning, images, music).
- **V2 Logos linked by arrows**: Arrows draw between logo tiles to show a relationship or a switch.
- **V3 One chunky word**: A single big title word on a question or punchline ("Why?", "Plot twist").
- **V4 Logo shuffle**: Logos flick rapidly in one slot, then the phrase lands ("every week").
- **V5 Comparison stack**: Product chips stack in one by one with price and who it's for.
- **V6 Big payoff word**: One huge bold word across the frame at the payoff moment.
- **V7 Kinetic text card**: Full-screen sentence builds word by word with inline emoji icons.
- **V8 Quote card**: When you quote what people say, their words type on in quote marks in the top band.
- **V9 Real-life montage**: Full-screen cut to real event and life clips on the emotional line.
- **V10 Flat sponsor UI**: The sponsor flow as simple flat cards (comment, DM, email). No glow, no 3D.
- **V11 Progress header**: A header that steps through the list or ladder and stays up, so viewers know where they are.
- **V12 Logo bloom pop**: Official logo + wordmark pops up with a soft white bloom, slight overshoot and a glint sweep, magic-reveal sound. No card around it.
- **V13 iOS ring timer**: Frosted ring timer in the open middle of the frame, counts up to the number you say in about 2 seconds, then goes.
- **V14 Calendar widget**: White iOS widget pops up and flips the year back for time jumps ("10+ years ago"). Gets its own clean moment on you.
- **V15 iPhone notification**: A real-feeling iOS banner slides down with the line you just said ("Report sent to the team"). Kept narrow inside the safe zone.
- **V16 Control Center tiles**: iPhone Wi-Fi and Phone tiles switch off on the words ("no internet, no phone").
- **V17 Gradient comment CTA**: White card with an Instagram-gradient ring pops right before the keyword, types "Comment" then a big gradient keyword, then holds. Nothing else in the card.
- **V18 Trip polaroids**: Your real trip or event photos drop in as polaroids with a camera flash and shutter click.
- **V19 Live profile CTA**: A dark Instagram screen in front of your chest: the keyword types into the comments and posts with a heart, then it swaps to your profile and the Follow button taps.
- **V20 Numbered effect counter**: A fixed series title up top ("10 AI tools") with a small 01/10 counter over a big yellow label for each item, so viewers always know where they are.
- **V21 Handwritten arrow notes**: Little handwritten caps notes ("before") with a drawn arrow pointing at the card. Feels like you are annotating your own work.
- **V22 Pinned comment banner**: One small handwritten line pinned at the top for the whole video (your call to action), so it works from second one.
- **V23 Results grid**: Two rows of results stacked ("New Video" over "Raw Clip"), swapping every second to show it works on many examples.

## B · Animated b-roll

Recreated app moments. Swap out anything private.

- **B1 Chat question + reply**: Your message types and sends, the bot reply streams in.
- **B2 Bots group chat**: Bots talking to each other with names, avatars and @mentions.
- **B3 Chat to Calendar**: Link clicked, Google Calendar opens, the event drops in.
- **B4 Chat to Drive to Sheets**: Link clicked, Drive opens, spreadsheet rows pop in.
- **B5 iPhone group chat**: iMessage-style chat inside an iPhone frame.
- **B6 Mac Dock click**: Zoom into the Dock, click, app bounces open.
- **B7 Notification ping**: Character avatar pings and a speech bubble pops out over your footage.
- **B8 Logo lockup pill**: Dark glass pill: app icon, name, "by" company logo, shine sweep.
- **B9 Character intro**: Character faces pop in a row with name tags.
- **B10 Concept diagram**: Simple animated explainer (cloud computer, accounts wiring up).
- **B11 Cinematic logo reveal**: Official footage, slow-mo and graded, logos glide in.

## X · Signature effects

One-second showpiece effects. One per idea, never stacked.

- **X1 Orbit**: Small rounded phone-screen cards (your other reels) circle around you, passing behind and in front of your body. You're cut out so they wrap you.
- **X2 Couch wall**: The wall behind you fills with a tiled collage of your past thumbnails, tweets and screenshots. You stay cut out in front.
- **X3 UI world**: Your background is swapped for a clean off-white chat app, and the prompt bar types your request right above your head.
- **X4 Frame break**: Your shot shrinks into a rounded phone frame on a dark blurred fill, and your body pokes out over the frame edge (3D pop-out).
- **X5 Digital zoom**: Fast push from the wide shot into a tight face crop on the key word, slight vignette. Hard snap, no blur.
- **X6 Phone mockup slide-in**: A dark phone mockup slides in from the frame edge beside you, a step list fills and a yellow button taps to "Done".
- **X7 Checklist ticks**: Small dark card in the empty wall beside your head, three steps tick green one by one as you say them.
- **X8 Headline wall**: Paper notes, quote cards and search-bar snippets stick onto the wall around your head, slightly tilted, one per beat.
- **X9 Zoom-through cut**: Camera pushes hard into a dark part of the frame (your jacket, a shadow), goes black, and the next scene opens out of it.
- **X10 Chart grow**: A glowing line chart draws across the room behind you, then shoots up in neon yellow with a glow trail on the payoff number.
- **X11 Pull-back reveal**: Your shot pulls back to reveal it's playing inside a Claude window, with a "Working on" task list ticking and a render bar filling beside it.
- **X12 Green screen double**: You at the bottom, and a smaller dark card above showing the same take from another angle, like a screen behind you.
- **X13 Paper cut**: You become a white-border paper cutout on a torn-paper collage with ransom-note letters, crayon scribbles and tape.
- **X14 Doodle boil**: A wobbly hand-drawn white outline traces around you and boils frame to frame, with doodled hearts and sparkles popping by your head.
- **X15 Vertigo**: Dolly zoom: you stay the same size while the room behind you stretches and pulls. Use on a reveal line.
- **X16 Cover story**: Your frame turns into a magazine cover: big thin serif masthead behind your head, small cover lines in the corners, italic serif strapline below.
- **X17 Glass type**: A huge frosted-glass word sits behind your head and refracts the room through it.
- **X18 Icon flashcards**: Inside the After card, cream flashcards flip through with one glossy 3D object each (typewriter = Captions, camera = B-roll, film reel = Animation) and a serif label.
- **X19 Monitor in a dark room**: The whole explainer plays on a monitor in a dark room with a glowing keyboard, simple line-art on screen and a fixed serif title below.

## M · Motion + pace

How the edit moves.

- **M1 Punch-in cuts**: Hard cut zooms at sentence starts so the shot keeps changing.
- **M2 Zoom-blur transitions**: Quick zoom-blur into and out of cutaways, soft warm light leak.
- **M3 Fast visuals**: A new visual every 2 to 3 seconds, never the same device twice in a row.
- **M4 Calm and still**: Few cuts, slow pushes, more breathing room. Fewer, stronger graphics.
- **M5 Two-framing jump cuts**: One take, alternating wide and tight framing on every sentence, pauses cut tight. Hard cuts only.
- **M6 Face-centred zoom**: Quick 115-120% punch-in on short emphasis lines, slow push-in that eases back on longer lines. Always centres your face.
- **M7 Colour circle wipe**: A brand-colour circle grows out of the card to wipe to the next section. Clean, no blur.

## S · Sound

Sound design, mixed to about -14 LUFS, always under your voice.

- **S1 SFX on every visual**: Pops, whooshes, ticks and hits on every graphic, always under your voice.
- **S2 Light SFX**: Effects only on the big moments.
- **S3 Music bed**: A quiet background track under your voice. Use music you have the rights to.

## G · Colour + finish

The overall look and colour.

- **G1 Warm cinematic**: Light warm grade, vignette, blurred room behind cards.
- **G2 Clean light**: Soft white panels, thin accent bars, no emoji or badges.
- **G3 Soft blue pastel**: Pastel blue canvas, playful stickers.
- **G4 Apple clean**: White iOS cards, frosted glass, SF Pro, soft shadows. One screen per beat. No glow text, stamps or neon.
- **G5 Cool girl warm**: Soft warm sepia grade, cream text, one butter-yellow serif italic punch line with twinkling sparkles. Pairs with the Orbit effect.
- **G6 Cool dude editorial**: Room darkened, you lifted so you stay lit, teal tone and vignette, no grain. Wide shot alternates with a tight close-up like a studio interview.
- **G7 Flat yellow canvas**: Whole video sits on one flat butter-yellow canvas with white text and white-border cards. Instantly recognisable series look.
